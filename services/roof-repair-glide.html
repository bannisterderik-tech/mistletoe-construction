What Glide roofs are up against

Glide is where the valley ends and the mountains begin. Sitting east of Roseburg along Highway 138, at higher elevation in the forested North Umpqua corridor, it is wetter and colder than the valley floor — on the order of 45-plus inches of precipitation a year, and unlike the towns down on I-5, Glide actually sees meaningful snow and freeze-thaw in winter. That adds a whole category of roof problems the lowland towns rarely deal with: ice at the eaves, snow load, and freeze-thaw working at every crack.

Then there is the forest itself. Homes tucked among the firs toward Idleyld Park and the Colliding Rivers are deeply shaded and heavily debris-loaded, so moss and packed needles are relentless. And since the 2020 Archie Creek Fire tore through the North Umpqua canyon, wildfire resilience is on a lot of Glide homeowners' minds when they think about their roof.

The repairs we do most in Glide

  • Ice-dam and eave leaks. Freeze-thaw at the eaves backs water under the shingles — a cold-country problem we address with proper ice-and-water membrane.
  • Snow-load and flashing stress. Wet snow and its melt work chimney and valley flashing hard; we re-flash the failures.
  • Heavy moss and forest debris. Deeply shaded North Umpqua lots grow moss fast and pack valleys with needles.
  • Wildfire-area concerns. After Archie Creek, more homeowners ask about ember-resistant details and Class A materials.

How a Glide roof repair works

Glide's cold-country roofs add a step to the diagnosis: besides the usual rain leaks, we look for freeze-thaw and ice-dam damage at the eaves, where meltwater backs up under the shingles in a way lowland roofs rarely see. We photograph the source, write you a clear price, and photograph the finished repair.

Our fixes are built for the mountains — proper ice-and-water membrane where the eaves and valleys need it, re-flashing that stands up to snow melt, and moss treatment on the deeply shaded North Umpqua planes that grow it fastest. And where a homeowner is thinking about the next fire season after Archie Creek, we can talk through ember-resistant details as part of the work. Same honest scope, whatever the elevation.

Permits and building for the mountains

Minor repairs generally need no permit in Douglas County; a full reroof does, and overlays are not allowed, so replacement is a complete tear-off. In Glide's climate that tear-off is a chance to add the cold-weather details lowland roofs skip — wider ice-and-water shield at the eaves and in the valleys.
