What the canyon does to a roof

Canyonville earns its name — it sits at the mouth of the steep Canyon Creek drainage, right where the South Umpqua valley begins its climb toward the Cow Creek and Siskiyou grades. That location makes it noticeably wetter than the valley floor to the north, on the order of 43 inches of rain a year, and the surrounding timbered slopes keep many homes shaded and slow to dry.

Steep terrain also means steep, complex rooflines and a lot of tree cover. Fir needles and forest debris pile into valleys and gutters, moss thrives on the shaded planes, and the heavy rain finds any weak flashing detail. Canyonville is classic moss-and-debris leak country, with the added wrinkle of steeper, harder-to-access roofs.

The repairs we do most in Canyonville

  • Moss and debris leaks. Shaded, timber-ringed lots grow moss fast and pack valleys with needles — the top cause of Canyonville roof failures.
  • Valley leaks on complex roofs. Steep, cut-up rooflines have more valleys to fail; we re-flash and clear them.
  • Heavy-rain flashing failures. The extra rainfall exposes any tired chimney, skylight or sidewall flashing.
  • Gutter overflow. Forest debris clogs gutters and backs water under the eaves, rotting fascia.
