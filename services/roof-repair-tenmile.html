Tenmile's setting — wet, forested foothill country

Tenmile is a rural unincorporated community southwest of Roseburg, spread along Highway 42 — the old Coos Bay Wagon Road route — as it climbs from the Umpqua Valley into the Coast Range on the way to the coast. This is timber-and-forest country: homes on wooded acreage tucked into the foothills, near neighbors like Camas Valley and Brockway, on the wetter, greener side of the county. As the land rises toward the coast, the rain and fog thicken, and that makes for a demanding roof environment.

The wider Umpqua pattern of wet winters and dry summers still applies, but Tenmile's Coast Range position pushes the wet side harder. Marine systems dump more rain here than on the I-5 floor, fog lingers in the folds of the hills, and the deep conifer canopy keeps roof planes damp. The result is heavy, fast-growing moss, needle-packed valleys, and overflowing gutters as the defining roof problems.

The repairs we do most in Tenmile

  • Heavy moss and forest debris. The wet foothill shade grows moss fast; it lifts shingle edges and traps water, and needles pack the valleys. We clear, repair, and treat to slow regrowth.
  • Clogged gutters and eave rot. Heavy conifer debris overflows gutters; the backed-up water rots fascia and sheathing.
  • Flashing failures. Chimneys, sidewalls, and vents are the leading leak source on forested homes.
  • Rural and outbuilding roofs. Garages, shops, and barns leak too — we repair the whole property.
  • Wind and storm damage. Coast Range storms peel tabs on exposed slopes; we replace and re-seal.

How a Tenmile roof repair works

We trace the leak to its real source before quoting — on steep, forested rooflines the stain is rarely under the actual failure, so we inspect from the attic and the roof, photograph what we find, and give you a clear written price up front. When the repair is done, you get before-and-after photos, useful for your records and for insurance.

Because moisture and moss drive so many leaks in this wet corner of the county, our repairs are built to shed water and stay dry: correctly lapped flashing, sealed penetrations, and moss treatment on the deeply shaded planes that grow it fastest. Where clogged gutters are feeding the problem — nearly always the case up here — we handle the drainage as part of the fix.

Do you drive out to Tenmile for roof repairs?

Yes. Tenmile sits southwest of Roseburg on Highway 42 toward Coos Bay, about 35 to 40 minutes from our Riddle shop, and it is inside our service area. Call or text (541) 670-5005.

Why do Tenmile roofs grow so much moss?

Tenmile sits in the Coast Range foothills where marine air pushes in along the Highway 42 corridor, so it runs wetter, foggier, and greener than the I-5 valley, and homes are heavily shaded by conifers. Damp, shaded planes are ideal for moss, which lifts shingle edges and holds water against the deck.

Can you repair roofs on rural and forested Tenmile properties?

Yes. Tenmile is forested foothill country with homes on wooded acreage, often with garages, shops, and outbuildings. We repair the whole property — asphalt, metal, and low-slope roofs — and give you one honest scope.
