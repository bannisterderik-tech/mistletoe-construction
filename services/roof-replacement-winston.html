Winston is best known as the home of the Wildlife Safari, but for roofers it's known for something else: a valley that mixes river humidity with open, wind-exposed lots. The town spreads out where the Lookingglass River meets the South Umpqua, then climbs toward Lookingglass Road and the Dillard flats. That range of terrain — sheltered riverside neighborhoods and breezy hillside acreage — means Winston roofs don't all age the same way, and a good replacement has to account for both.

Wind, water, and Winston roofs

Two things drive roof wear here. First, the wet season: Douglas County pulls in roughly 30-plus inches of rain from fall through spring, and the valley bottoms hold morning humidity along the rivers. Shaded and north-facing planes stay damp, and moss settles in. Second, exposure: homes out toward Lookingglass and on the open flats catch more wind than sheltered in-town lots, and wind-driven rain finds every weak flashing joint and lifted shingle edge. That's exactly where cheap 3-tab roofs and reused flashing fail first.

Summer flips to hot and dry, baking the sun-facing slopes and accelerating granule loss. The oaks and firs that shade Winston yards also fill gutters every fall. Put it together and you get the familiar Douglas County pattern — roofs that look fine from the street quietly wearing out from the top down.

Signs your Winston roof is ready

  • Lifted or missing shingles after a windy storm, especially on exposed lots.
  • Granules in the gutters and thinning, shiny patches on the south slope.
  • Moss on the shaded, river-facing planes.
  • Leaks around chimneys, skylights, or sidewalls — usually a flashing problem on older homes.
  • 20-plus years of age on the many mid-century ranch homes across town.

If it's isolated wind damage, a repair may be all you need — and if a storm caused it, our storm-damage and insurance guide walks through documenting a claim.

Permits and the no-overlay rule

Winston reroofs follow Douglas County's rules: a permit for full replacements, no overlays, and kick-out flashing at sidewalls. We take care of the paperwork and inspections. On wind-exposed homes, the tear-off is doubly valuable because it lets us re-nail and re-flash to the correct wind spec instead of trusting a decades-old install. Our permit guide has the details.

Winston roof replacement FAQs

How much does a new roof cost in Winston?

Most asphalt reroofs run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off; metal runs 2–3× that. Our Douglas County cost guide covers the drivers; a free written estimate gives your exact number.

My shingles blew off in a windstorm — repair or replace?

If the roof is otherwise sound, we repair it. If the wind exposed widespread aging or brittle shingles, replacement with a higher wind-rated shingle is the smarter spend. We'll tell you honestly which it is.
