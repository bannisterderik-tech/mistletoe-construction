Local Knowledge

Why Roseburg roofs grow moss.

Roseburg sits in the hundred-valleys country of the Umpqua, where the weather works roofs from both directions. Roughly 33 inches of rain falls October through May, then July and August bake shingles at 90°F with almost no rain at all. That wet-dry whiplash is exactly the environment moss loves — long damp seasons to establish, then a dormant summer that hides the damage until fall.

The tree canopy that makes neighborhoods like Laurelwood, Hucrest, and the Mill-Pine district so pleasant is the other half of the problem. North-facing and shaded roof planes under oak and Douglas-fir stay damp for days after a rain, and the needle-and-leaf litter those trees drop feeds moss and clogs gutters every autumn.

  • Shaded north planes — the roof faces that never dry out fully in winter are where Roseburg moss takes hold first
  • Oak & fir debris — canopy litter holds moisture against shingles and dams valleys
  • Historic downtown homes — older, steeper roofs in the Mill-Pine and downtown districts need careful, hand-tool work
  • Clogged gutters — moss and needles back up water at the eaves, so gutter cleaning goes hand-in-hand with moss removal
  • Lifted shingle edges — established moss roots wedge under shingles and open paths for wind-driven rain

Do you actually work in Roseburg, or just advertise here?

We work in Roseburg constantly — our shop in Riddle is about 20 minutes down I-5, and Roseburg is our busiest market. This is our home county, not a pin on a franchise map from Eugene.

Why is moss so bad on Roseburg's tree-lined streets?

Neighborhoods like Laurelwood and Hucrest sit under oak and Douglas-fir canopy that shades north-facing roof planes and drops moisture-holding debris. Shaded, damp shingles plus 33 inches of winter rain is the ideal moss nursery — which is why we usually pair moss removal with gutter cleaning.

Should I ever pressure wash my Roseburg roof?

No. Pressure washing strips the protective granules off asphalt shingles, forces water under the edges, and can void your warranty — it makes the roof look clean for one summer while shortening its life by years. We remove moss by hand and treat the roots instead.
