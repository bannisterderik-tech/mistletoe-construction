Local Knowledge

Green's shaded, settled lots grow roof moss.

Green is the unincorporated community wedged between Roseburg and Winston, straddling Highway 99 and the South Umpqua just off I-5. It's one of the more densely settled residential areas in the county outside the city limits, with a lot of established homes, mature landscaping, and tree-shaded lots — the kind of setting where roofs stay damp on their shaded sides and moss quietly takes hold.

Being tucked along the river between two larger towns, Green gets the same 30-plus inches of annual rain and the same wet-winter, dry-summer cycle as Roseburg, plus a touch of the extra river-bottom humidity that Winston sees. The result is reliable moss growth on north-facing planes and under overhanging trees, especially on the older ranch-style roofs common through the district.

  • Mature landscaping — established trees shade roofs and drop moisture-holding debris
  • River-bottom humidity — the South Umpqua corridor keeps the air damp
  • North-facing planes — the roof faces that miss winter sun grow moss first
  • Older ranch roofs — the low-slope roofs common in Green hold moisture and moss
  • Gutter debris — leaf litter clogs eaves, so gutter cleaning pairs with moss removal

Our Method

Hand removal. Treatment. Prevention. Never pressure washing.

A pressure washer makes a mossy Green roof look great for one summer — while blasting off the granules your shingles need to survive the next twenty winters. We don't own one for roof work, on purpose.

01

Inspect & photograph

We come out to the Green district to map the moss across the roof, check the shaded planes for damage underneath, and show you photos before we quote.

02

Gentle hand removal

Soft brushes and hand tools lift the moss mat without tearing granules or lifting shingles — slower than a pressure washer, and far kinder to your roof.

03

Treat what remains

A moss treatment kills the roots and spores the brushes can't reach, so regrowth starts from zero, not from a head start.

04

Prevent regrowth

Zinc strips at the ridge inhibit new moss with every rain, and our prevention guide covers the seasonal debris-clearing that keeps a Green roof clear.

Typical Green-district jobs run about $250–$600 depending on roof size, pitch, and how established the moss is; heavier growth or steep roofs cost more. We provide a written price after inspecting the roof.
