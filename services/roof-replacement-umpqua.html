What Umpqua weather does to a roof

Down in the river valley, Umpqua holds the damp. Morning fog settles over the water, the tree canopy along the banks shades north-facing roof planes, and the region's 30-plus inches of winter rain soaks everything from October into spring. That keeps roofs wet long enough for moss and lichen to establish, especially on the river-facing side of a house. Then the valley's hot, dry summers bake the exposed slopes and wear granules off the south faces. Riverside humidity plus that wet-dry swing is hard on asphalt, and it is why Umpqua roofs often need attention sooner than a generic lifespan chart would suggest.

The mature trees along the Umpqua also drop needles and leaves into gutters and valleys every fall. On the wooded river lots, packed valleys and overflowing gutters back water up under the shingles and rot the roof edge — a quiet, common way an Umpqua roof begins to fail.

Signs an Umpqua roof is ready for replacement

  • Heavy moss on shaded, river-facing slopes — lifting shingles and holding water against the deck.
  • Granules in the gutters after the dry summer months.
  • Curling, cupping, or brittle shingles on the sun-exposed faces.
  • Debris-packed valleys and overflowing gutters on wooded river lots.
  • Failed flashing at chimneys and sidewalls on older farmsteads.
  • Recurring leaks, or a roof past 20 years — the point where replacing usually beats patching.

Not every Umpqua roof needs replacing yet. If a roof repair is the honest answer, that is what we will tell you — and the Oregon roof-lifespan guide helps you judge where yours is.

Umpqua permits and the no-overlay rule

Douglas County does not allow shingle-over-shingle overlays anywhere in the county, Umpqua included. Every reroof here is a full tear-off, and jobs over 100 square feet generally require a permit with kick-out flashing at sidewalls. That costs a little more than a lay-over would in some other state — and it is genuinely better, because your deck actually gets inspected and any rot gets fixed instead of buried under a second layer. We handle the paperwork and inspections; our Douglas County roofing permit guide walks through the details if you like to know what is happening.
