On the Roof

What we actually check in a free roof inspection.

No clipboard theatrics, no scare tactics — just what our family crew looks at when we climb a Douglas County roof, and the honest report you get whether or not you ever hire us.

By Mistletoe Construction Oregon CCB #255729 Published June 26, 2026 6 min read

⚡ The short version

A real free inspection is four passes: the ground and eaves, the gutters and downspouts, the attic from inside, and the roof itself. We photograph everything, then give you one of three plain answers — maintain, repair, or replace. If your roof has years left, we'll tell you so. That's the whole point of doing it for free.

Let's be honest about why "free roof inspection" makes people nervous. Too many of them are really a sales appointment in a costume — somebody walks the roof for four minutes, comes down shaking their head, and starts talking about a full replacement before they've even opened the attic hatch. We're a family-owned company based right here in Riddle, and we've heard the stories from neighbors all over the Umpqua Valley. So here's exactly what our version looks like, step by step, with nothing hidden.

The goal isn't to find you a reason to buy a roof. It's to give you a clear, photographed picture of what shape your roof is actually in, so you can make the call on your own timeline. Sometimes that means we spend an hour on a Roseburg rooftop and tell the owner to call us again in five years.

1. The ground walk and the eaves

Before anyone gets on a ladder, we walk the perimeter of the house. A surprising amount of a roof's story is readable from the yard. We're sighting along the ridgeline and the roof planes looking for sag, waves, or dips that hint at tired decking underneath. We check the eaves and fascia for peeling paint, soft spots, and the tell-tale staining that means water has been running where it shouldn't. We look at the soffit vents, the drip edge, and any spot where two roof planes meet a wall — the places that leak first in our climate.

We also just look up. Curling or cupping shingle edges, dark algae streaks, bald patches where the granules have washed away, and moss lines on the north-facing slopes are all visible from below if you know what you're looking at. On a lot of Douglas County homes tucked under Douglas firs, that shaded north slope tells us more than the rest of the roof combined.

2. The gutters and downspouts

Gutters are a roof's diagnostic tray, and most people never think to read them. We check what's collecting in there. A little grit is normal shed. But cupfuls of sandy granules — that's shingle armor washing off, and on a roof past fifteen years it's one of the clearest signs the surface is wearing out. We follow the water: are the gutters pitched right, are the downspouts actually carrying water away from the foundation, is anything overflowing behind the fascia and rotting it quietly. Fir needles and moss clog runs faster here than almost anywhere, so we note whether the whole system is keeping up with an Oregon October. Our gutter guide for Oregon rain goes deeper on why this matters.

3. The attic — the most honest room in the house

This is the step the four-minute crews skip, and it's the one that tells the truth. With a flashlight, from inside, we look up at the underside of the roof deck. Pinpoints of daylight, dark water staining on the sheathing, damp or matted insulation, rusted nail tips, or a musty smell all mean water is getting past the surface — sometimes for years before it ever shows on your ceiling. We check the ventilation too, because a poorly vented attic rots a roof from below in winter and bakes it in summer; our attic ventilation guide explains that whole quiet failure. Ten minutes in the attic saves a lot of guessing on the roof.

4. The roof itself

Now we go up — tied off, carefully, because a wet mossy Oregon roof is genuinely dangerous to walk. On the surface we're checking a running list: shingle condition across the whole field (not just the easy-to-reach edge), how much life is left in the granule layer, and whether shingles have gone brittle enough to crack when we lift a tab. We inspect every penetration — pipe boots, vents, the chimney, skylights — because rubber boots dry out and crack long before the shingles give up. We look hard at the flashing in the valleys and along the sidewalls and chimney, since that's where the majority of real leaks start. And we check the ridge, the hip lines, and any prior repairs to see whether earlier work was done right or just smeared over with tar.

If there's moss, we assess whether it's early and treatable or whether it's established enough to be lifting shingle edges and holding water against the deck. That distinction changes everything about the recommendation.

Repair, replace, or leave it alone?

One problem on an otherwise healthy roof under fifteen years old is almost always a repair. A stack of issues on a roof past twenty usually points to replacement. And a roof that's simply doing fine gets a clean bill and a note to check it again next year. If you want to see the exact list a thorough inspection covers, our roof inspection checklist lays it all out.

The report you actually get

When we come down, you don't get a vague verdict and a pressure pitch. You get photos of what we found — the good and the bad — and a straight recommendation in plain English: maintain, repair, or replace, with the reasoning behind it. If it's a repair, we'll tell you roughly what it involves. If it's replacement, we'll explain why patching wouldn't pencil out. And if your roof is fine, you'll hear that too, because a five-star reputation in a county this size is worth more to us than one roof sold under false pretenses.

You can also skip ahead and get a fast ballpark before we even show up. Our instant estimate gives you a rough number in a couple of minutes, and the on-roof inspection confirms it.

How long does it take, and what do I do?

Most single-family homes take about 45 minutes to an hour, depending on pitch, complexity, and how much moss we're wading through. You don't need to be up there with us — you're welcome to follow along on the ground, but many folks just hand us attic access and get back to their day. We serve all of Douglas County from Riddle, so scheduling is usually quick. If you'd like a sense of timing, our guide on how long a roof inspection takes covers it.

One honest note on the offer

Right now every new roof we install includes $500 off, plus free whole-home gutters on qualifying roofs ($20k+). We mention it because it's real and it's genuinely a good time if it does turn out to be time. But the inspection stands on its own — it's free whether the answer is "replace it" or "you've got seven good years left, call us then." We'd rather earn the roof you actually need than sell you one you don't.

If it's been more than a couple of years since anyone looked at your roof — or if a storm just rolled through and you're wondering — get on the calendar. It costs nothing and it beats letting the next atmospheric river pick the timing for you.
