Local Knowledge

What Tenmile roofs are up against.

Tenmile spreads along Highway 42 and Tenmile Creek west of Winston, where the open inland valley begins to fold up into the wooded foothills of the Coast Range on the way toward Camas Valley and the Coquille country. That transition zone gives Tenmile a slightly harsher climate than roofs a few miles east on the valley floor — more wind off the ridges and more moisture rolling in from the coast.

The extra wind is the difference-maker here. Exposed hillside and ridge lots catch gusts that work under shingle edges and loosen fasteners over time, so edge and ridge detailing matters as much as the shingle itself. The damp, cooler air keeps shaded, north-facing planes wet longer, feeding moss that lifts shingle edges and holds water against the deck. Fir and oak canopy fills gutters each fall, and because Tenmile is rural farm-and-timber country, many properties carry older homes, barns, and shops with roofs long past due for replacement.

  • Wind exposure — lifted shingle edges on ridge and hillside lots near the Coast Range
  • Moss on shaded planes — cooler, damper coastal-edge air keeping roofs wet
  • Heavy gutter debris — fir and oak leaf-and-needle fall each autumn
  • Rural barns & older homes — aging roofs and outbuildings ideal for durable metal

Roofing built for coastal-edge weather

A roof near the Coast Range needs both wind resistance and moisture defense, so we build for both. When we replace a roof in Tenmile, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing for rot from years of moss or gutter overflow, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and around penetrations, upgraded edge and ridge fastening for the wind, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles rated to hold up in gusty, wet conditions.

Because Tenmile takes more wind than the valley floor, metal roofing is an especially strong option here — it locks down tight, sheds debris and moss, and lasts for decades on both homes and outbuildings. If your roof still has life left, we'll tell you honestly; plenty of Tenmile calls are really moss removal and gutter work. Our moss-prevention guide explains how to keep a damp, shaded roof healthy between visits.

Tenmile roofing FAQs

Do you serve Tenmile?

Yes. Tenmile is along Highway 42 west of Winston toward Camas Valley, and it's part of our regular Douglas County service area.

What roof problems are common in Tenmile?

Wind-lifted edges on exposed coastal-edge lots, moss on damp shaded planes, heavy fall gutter debris from fir and oak, and older rural homes and barns with aging roofs due for a full tear-off.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Tenmile?

Most asphalt reroofs run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off; metal typically runs 2–3× that and is popular on barns. Right now every new roof gets $500 off plus free gutters on qualifying $20k+ jobs. See our Douglas County cost guide.

Do Tenmile reroofs need a permit?

Yes — reroofs require a permit and Douglas County doesn't allow overlays, so a full tear-off is required. We pull the permit and handle inspections for you.
