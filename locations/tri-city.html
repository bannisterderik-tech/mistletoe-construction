Local Knowledge

What Tri City roofs are up against.

Tri City sits on the South Umpqua valley floor just south of Myrtle Creek, strung along Old Highway 99 with I-5 running past. It grew up as the meeting point of a few small communities — hence the name — and today it's a mix of established residential streets and rural lots on the valley edges. Because it shares the South Umpqua climate with our home base in Riddle just to the south, we know these roofs about as well as we know our own.

The southern Umpqua Valley works roofs from both directions. Winters are long and wet, and the oaks and firs around Tri City throw shade that keeps north-facing planes damp — prime moss conditions. The same trees fill gutters with leaves and needles every fall. Then summer flips the script: hot, dry 90°F-plus stretches bake the sunny slopes and strip granules off the shingles. A lot of Tri City's older homes along Old Highway 99 are on roofs that have quietly reached the end of their service life, and some carry a second layer that has to come off before a proper replacement can go on.

  • Moss on shaded planes — north sides under oak and fir canopy
  • Granule loss — sunny slopes baked by hot, dry South Umpqua summers
  • Clogged gutters — oak and fir debris backing water up under the eaves
  • Aging & layered roofs — older Old Highway 99 homes due for a full tear-off

Roofing done right, right next door

Being minutes away lets us do Tri City work the way it should be done — attentively and without the rush of a crew that's an hour from home. When we replace a roof here, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing for any rot hidden under old layers or moss, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and around penetrations, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles built for the South Umpqua's wet winters and hot summers. On the shaded, moss-prone slopes we add zinc or copper strips and a simple maintenance rhythm.

If your roof still has years left, we'll say so — a lot of Tri City calls come down to moss removal and gutter work rather than a full replacement. And for the homes and shops that want a roof they'll never think about again, metal roofing is worth a look. Our Oregon roof-lifespan guide can help you gauge where yours stands.
