Local Knowledge

What Days Creek roofs are up against.

Days Creek strings out along Highway 227 and the South Umpqua River east of Canyonville and Myrtle Creek, a small, close-knit timber community wrapped in steep, forested hills. It's the kind of place where the school is the heart of town and the river runs through everyone's summer. That deep forest setting is beautiful — and it's the single biggest factor in how roofs age here.

Heavy Douglas-fir and mixed hardwood canopy throws deep shade over roofs and keeps north-facing planes damp long after the rain quits, so moss is the number-one problem. Established moss lifts shingle edges and holds water against the deck. The same trees bury gutters in needles and leaves every fall, and clogged downspouts push water back under the eaves. Homes on the canyon and hill lots catch wind that lifts shingle edges, and because this is an older, rural corridor, many houses and outbuildings carry roofs well past their service life. Sitting up a forested river canyon, Days Creek homeowners are also increasingly asking about fire-resistant roofing.

  • Moss on shaded planes — the top issue under heavy forest canopy
  • Gutter overload — fir needles and leaf debris clogging downspouts every fall
  • Wind-lifted edges — canyon and hillside lots along the 227 corridor
  • Aging rural roofs & wildfire questions — older homes due for tear-off; growing interest in fire-rated roofing

Roofing for forested river country

A roof surrounded by big firs has to shed debris and fight moss for its whole life, so that's how we build them. When we replace a roof in Days Creek, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing for rot hidden under moss or old layers, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and at penetrations, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles. On the moss-prone north slopes we add zinc or copper strips and a maintenance rhythm to keep it from creeping back.

For homes and shops tucked up the forested canyon, metal roofing is often the smart long-term choice — it sheds needles and moss, holds up in wind, and lasts for decades. And for anyone concerned about fire in this corridor, our wildfire-resistant roofing guide covers the Class A assemblies that make sense in the wooded parts of Douglas County. If your roof still has life left, we'll tell you honestly — a lot of Days Creek calls are really moss removal and gutter work.
