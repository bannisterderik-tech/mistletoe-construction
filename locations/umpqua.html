Local Knowledge

What Umpqua roofs are up against.

The community of Umpqua lies northwest of Roseburg along Highway 138 West, where the Umpqua River winds through some of the oldest vineyard ground in Oregon. It's a landscape of tasting rooms, rolling vine-covered hills, riverside homes, and rural acreage — beautiful country that puts two very different stresses on a roof at the same time.

Down along the river, humidity and shade from cottonwoods, oaks, and firs keep north-facing roof planes damp, and moss and algae streaking follow. Established moss lifts shingle edges and traps water against the deck, quietly aging a roof from below. Up on the open vineyard slopes, it's the opposite problem — the Umpqua Valley's hot, dry summers bake asphalt at 90°F-plus for weeks, stripping the granules that protect the shingle. Riverside trees also load gutters heavily each fall, and the area's many older homes and winery outbuildings often carry roofs that are simply past their prime.

  • Moss and algae — the top issue on shaded, humid riverside planes
  • Granule loss — sun-baked vineyard slopes after dry Umpqua summers
  • Heavy gutter debris — cottonwood and oak leaf fall along the river
  • Winery buildings & older homes — larger structures and aging roofs due for attention

Roofing for river-and-vineyard country

A roof in Umpqua has to hold up to both river damp and vineyard sun, so we build it to answer both. When we replace a roof here, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing for moisture damage from years of moss and gutter overflow, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and around penetrations, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles rated to hold up under both moisture and UV. On shaded riverside slopes we add zinc or copper strips and a maintenance rhythm to keep moss from returning.

Umpqua is also full of larger structures — tasting rooms, production buildings, barns, and shops — where metal roofing earns its keep by shedding debris and moss and lasting for decades. Whether it's the house or a winery building, we'll walk you through the honest trade-offs between asphalt and metal for your specific structure and budget.

Do you serve Umpqua?

Yes. The community of Umpqua sits northwest of Roseburg along Highway 138 West and the river, in the valley's wine country, and it's part of our regular Douglas County service area.

What roof problems are common in Umpqua?

Moss and algae on shaded riverside planes, granule loss on sun-baked vineyard slopes, gutters clogged with cottonwood and oak, and older homes and winery buildings with aging roofs.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Umpqua?

Most asphalt reroofs run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off; metal typically runs 2–3× that and is popular on winery buildings. Right now every new roof gets $500 off plus free gutters on qualifying $20k+ jobs. See our Douglas County cost guide.

Can you roof a winery building in Umpqua?

Yes — alongside houses we handle metal and asphalt roofing on tasting rooms, production buildings, barns, and shops. Metal is a favorite for larger structures because it sheds debris and moss and lasts decades.
