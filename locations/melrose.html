Local Knowledge

What Melrose roofs are up against.

Melrose spreads west of Roseburg along Melrose Road and into the Garden Valley and Doerner country, a rolling landscape of vineyards, orchards, hobby farms, and rural home sites. It's one of the warmer, more open corners of the Umpqua Valley — the same conditions that make it good grape country make it demanding on a roof. Out here, homes sit on big, exposed lots rather than shaded neighborhood streets, so sun and wind, not just moss, drive most of the roofing work.

The Umpqua Valley's hot, dry summers bake south- and west-facing slopes at 90°F-plus for weeks, stripping granules off asphalt and turning shingles brittle. On the hilltop and ridge properties that catch the best vineyard views, wind gets under shingle edges and works them loose. Where oak and fir shade part of a roof or line a driveway, moss and gutter debris still show up in the wet season. And because Melrose is rural, a lot of properties carry older homes plus barns and shops with roofs that are well past due for replacement.

  • Sun and UV damage — granule loss and brittle shingles on open, west-facing slopes
  • Wind exposure — lifted shingle edges on hilltop and vineyard-view properties
  • Scattered moss — on shaded planes near oak stands and tree lines
  • Barns & outbuildings — rural shops and farm structures ideal for durable metal roofing

Roofing for open, rural Melrose lots

Roofs in Melrose take more sun and wind than shaded valley-floor homes, so we build them to match. When we replace a roof here, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing, then rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and at penetrations, upgraded edge and ridge fastening for wind resistance, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles rated to hold up under UV and gusts. On the open hilltop properties, that wind detailing matters as much as the shingle itself.

Melrose is also barn-and-shop country, and that's where metal roofing shines — it sheds debris, ignores moss, handles wind on exposed lots, and lasts decades with almost no upkeep. Whether it's the house, the shop, or a winery outbuilding, we'll lay out the honest trade-offs between asphalt and metal for your specific structure.

Melrose roofing FAQs

Do you serve Melrose?

Yes. Melrose is the rural, vineyard-dotted country just west of Roseburg, and it's a regular part of our Douglas County service area — about 30 minutes north of our Riddle shop.

What roof problems are common in Melrose?

Granule loss and curling on sun-baked slopes, wind-lifted edges on hilltop and vineyard-view lots, scattered moss near tree lines, clogged gutters, and aging roofs on older rural homes and outbuildings.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Melrose?

Most asphalt reroofs run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off; metal typically runs 2–3× that and is popular on shops and barns. Right now every new roof gets $500 off plus free gutters on qualifying $20k+ jobs. See our Douglas County cost guide.

Can you reroof a barn or winery building in Melrose?

Yes — we handle metal roofing on barns, shops, and winery outbuildings throughout the Melrose and Garden Valley area. Metal sheds debris and moss, handles wind on open lots, and lasts decades.
