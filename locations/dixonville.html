Local Knowledge

What Dixonville roofs are up against.

Dixonville sits just east of Roseburg where Highway 138 East begins its climb toward Glide, Idleyld Park, and the North Umpqua canyon. It's a rural community of homes tucked into rolling hills of oak and Douglas-fir, farmsteads along the creek bottoms, and newer places on view lots up the ridges. That mixed terrain means a Dixonville roof rarely faces just one enemy — it usually deals with sun, wind, and shade all at once.

On the south- and west-facing slopes, the Umpqua Valley's hot, dry summers bake asphalt for weeks, stripping granules and leaving shingles brittle. On the north sides shaded by oak and fir, the wet season keeps things damp long enough for moss to move in and lift shingle edges. Ridge and hilltop homes catch wind that works loose fasteners over time, and every fall the oaks fill gutters with leaves that back water up under the eaves. Because Dixonville is rural, many properties also carry older homes and outbuildings whose roofs are simply past their service life.

  • Granule loss — sun-baked south and west slopes after dry Umpqua summers
  • Moss on shaded planes — north sides under oak and fir canopy
  • Wind-lifted edges — ridge and hilltop lots along the 138 corridor
  • Clogged gutters & aging roofs — oak-leaf overload and older farmhouse roofs due for tear-off

Roofing for Dixonville's mixed terrain

The right roof for a Dixonville home has to answer sun, shade, and wind in one assembly. When we replace a roof here, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing for any rot from years of moss or gutter overflow, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and around penetrations, upgraded wind fastening for the ridge lots, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles. On the moss-prone north slopes we'll add zinc or copper strips and a maintenance rhythm so it doesn't creep back.

For the hilltop homes and rural shops that take the most weather, metal roofing is often the smart long-term play — it sheds debris and moss, handles wind, and lasts decades. If your roof still has life left, we'll tell you that too; a lot of Dixonville calls come down to moss removal and gutter work rather than a full replacement.

Do you serve Dixonville?

Yes. Dixonville is just east of Roseburg on Highway 138 East toward Glide, and it's part of our regular Douglas County service area — about 30–35 minutes north of our Riddle shop.
