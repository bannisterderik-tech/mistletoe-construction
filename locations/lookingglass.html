Local Knowledge

What Lookingglass roofs are up against.

The Lookingglass Valley sits southwest of Roseburg, between Winston and the Tenmile country, a low and fertile bowl of pasture and orchard ringed by wooded hills. Settlers gave it its name for the way sunlight glinted off the tall valley grass. It's one of the oldest-settled corners of Douglas County, and the housing reflects it — historic farmhouses, the old Lookingglass store and schoolhouse, working barns, and rural homes on acreage.

Ringed by hills and threaded with creeks, the valley floor holds moisture, which makes moss the number-one roofing concern here. Shaded, north-facing planes near the oaks and firs stay damp through the wet season, and established moss lifts shingle edges and holds water against the deck. The same trees drop a heavy load of leaves and needles that clog gutters every fall. On the open, sunny slopes, the Umpqua Valley's hot, dry summers still strip granules off asphalt. And with so many older farmhouses and outbuildings, a lot of Lookingglass roofs are simply at the end of their service life and due for a full tear-off.

  • Moss on shaded planes — the top issue on the damp, hill-ringed valley floor
  • Heavy gutter debris — oak and fir leaf-and-needle fall every autumn
  • Granule loss — sun-baked slopes after hot, dry Umpqua summers
  • Historic farmhouses & barns — aging or layered roofs due for replacement

Roofing for a historic farm valley

Older farmhouses reward careful work, and the damp valley floor demands a roof that fights moisture. When we replace a roof in Lookingglass, we tear off completely to the deck — Douglas County doesn't allow shingle-over-shingle overlays — inspect the sheathing on those older homes for any rot hidden under layers or moss, and rebuild with synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys and around penetrations, fresh flashing, and Owens Corning architectural shingles. On the shaded, moss-prone slopes we'll add zinc or copper strips and set up a maintenance rhythm so moss never gets a real foothold again.

Lookingglass is barn country, and for barns, shops, and equipment sheds, metal roofing is usually the right long-term answer — it sheds debris and moss and lasts for decades with almost no upkeep. If a roof still has years left, we'll say so; many valley calls are really moss removal and gutter work, and our moss-prevention guide explains how to keep a valley-floor roof healthy between visits.

Lookingglass roofing FAQs

Do you serve the Lookingglass Valley?

Yes. Lookingglass is the historic farm valley southwest of Roseburg, between Winston and Tenmile, and it's part of our regular Douglas County service area.

What roof problems are common in Lookingglass?

Moss on the damp, hill-ringed valley floor, heavy fall gutter debris from oak and fir, granule loss on sunny slopes, and aging roofs on the valley's many historic farmhouses and barns.

How much does a roof replacement cost in Lookingglass?

Most asphalt reroofs run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off; metal typically runs 2–3× that and is popular on barns. Right now every new roof gets $500 off plus free gutters on qualifying $20k+ jobs. See our Douglas County cost guide.

Can you reroof an old farmhouse or barn?

Yes — from careful tear-offs on historic homes to durable metal roofing on barns and shops. Douglas County doesn't allow overlays, so we tear off to the deck and inspect the sheathing on every replacement.
