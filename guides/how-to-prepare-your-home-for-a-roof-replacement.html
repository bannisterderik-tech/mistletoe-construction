Getting Ready

How to prepare your home for a roof replacement.

Roofing day goes smoothly when the home is ready for it. Here's the exact checklist our Riddle-based crew gives Douglas County homeowners — inside, outside, and the morning of — so nothing gets broken, dusted, or in the way.

⚡ Quick answer

Before roofing day: clear the driveway and the ground around the house for the dumpster and materials, move any vehicles you'll need, take down pictures, mirrors, and fragile shelf items (tear-off vibrates the whole structure), cover items stored in the attic and garage against dust, secure pets in a quiet interior room, and mow and clear the yard so we can sweep up stray nails afterward. You don't have to be home the whole time — roofing is all outside — but expect real noise, and plan for a quiet space if you work from home.

A roof replacement is a big, loud, one-to-three-day event, and a little prep the day before makes it go far better. We handle the heavy protection — tarping your landscaping, staging the dumpster carefully, sweeping every nail at the end — but there are things only you can do inside your own home. Here's the checklist we give every Douglas County homeowner, from Roseburg to Canyonville, before we tear off the first shingle.

Clear the driveway and the perimeter

The crew needs room to work. On roofing day a dumpster or dump trailer arrives, materials get delivered and staged near the house, and ladders lean against multiple walls. Move any vehicle you'll need out of the garage and driveway the night before — once the dumpster is placed and shingles are being loaded up, you may be boxed in. Walk the full perimeter of the house and move anything close to the walls: patio furniture, grills, potted plants, garden ornaments, hoses, and kids' toys. Everything within a few feet of the house is in the debris zone.

Protect landscaping and outdoor features

We tarp and shield beds, decks, walkways, and your AC unit before tear-off, but you know your yard best. Trim back any prized shrubs near the walls so ladders and tarps don't crush them, and move fragile potted plants well clear. If you have a delicate garden bed right against the house, tell us at the walkthrough so we can plan our staging and drop zones around it. In our tree-heavy Umpqua Valley neighborhoods, we also appreciate a heads-up on gate locations and any sprinkler heads hiding in the lawn.

Prep the inside — tear-off shakes the whole house

This is the step most homeowners forget, and the one that saves the most heartache. Pulling thousands of old nails and shingles off the deck sends a steady vibration down through the framing. Anything hanging or perched can walk off a nail or a shelf:

  • Take down wall art, mirrors, and framed photos, especially on the top floor and on shared walls.
  • Clear fragile items off open shelves and the tops of cabinets and hutches.
  • Cover anything stored in the attic with old sheets or plastic — dust and small debris can sift down between the sheathing boards during tear-off.
  • Cover shelved items in the garage and, ideally, park cars outside it for the day.
  • Secure ceiling fans and light fixtures that are already loose.

Plan for pets, kids, and people

Roofing is loud — genuinely, all-day loud. Nail guns, tear-off, compressors, and boots overhead. For skittish pets, set them up in a quiet interior room away from the work side of the house, or board them for the day. Let us know if you have dogs so the crew keeps gates latched and watches the yard. Keep kids clear of ladders, the dumpster, and the drop zones. If you work from home, plan to work elsewhere or expect to talk over a lot of noise; many homeowners simply go about their normal workday off-site.

Do you have to be home?

No. Roofing happens entirely outside, so you can go to work as usual. We do like to walk the roof with you at the start to confirm the scope, and again at the end for the final walkthrough and photos. If you can't be there for those, we'll coordinate by phone and send documentation.

Give the crew what they need to start

A few small things keep the morning moving: unlock the gate to any fenced side yard, make sure the crew can reach an exterior power outlet if needed, and clear a path to the attic access if we've flagged possible deck or ventilation work. If you have a security system or cameras, know that people will be moving around the property all day. And if there's parking sensitivity on your street — narrow lanes in some older Roseburg and Myrtle Creek neighborhoods — let us know so we plan the dumpster drop accordingly.

What happens on the day

Once you're prepped, our process is straightforward: protect and tarp, full tear-off to the deck (Douglas County doesn't allow overlays), inspect the decking and repair any rot, then dry-in with synthetic underlayment and ice-and-water shield, install new flashing, and lay Owens Corning architectural shingles. We finish with a full haul-away and run magnetic sweepers across the driveway, walkways, and yard to catch stray nails. Quick answers

How long should I plan for?

Most single-family homes in Douglas County take one to three days once materials arrive. Steep pitches, complex rooflines, or hidden deck repairs can add a day.

Should I move my cars?

Yes — out of the garage and driveway, and a little down the street. The dumpster and material staging will fill the driveway, and you don't want to be boxed in.

What about the mess afterward?

We haul away all old material and run magnetic sweeps for nails across the whole property. Covering attic and garage storage in advance handles the fine dust we can't reach.

Can you work around my schedule?

We serve all of Douglas County from Riddle and schedule around weather windows and your availability. Call (541) 670-5005 to set it up.
