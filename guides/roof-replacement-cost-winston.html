Winston sits in one of the warmer, sunnier pockets of the Umpqua Valley — locals call it the banana belt — but "sunny" doesn't mean easy on roofs. The intense hot-dry summer followed by a long wet winter is one of the harder cycles asphalt shingles face anywhere in Oregon. 2026 Winston price ranges

  • Architectural asphalt: most homes land $10,000–$25,000, full tear-off included. Compact homes near the town center dip below; larger homes spreading toward Lookingglass and Brockway with steep roofs go above.
  • Per square (100 sq ft): roughly $475–$775 installed — the low end for walkable ranch pitches, the high end for steep, harness-required roofs.
  • Metal: about 1.9× asphalt, and a strong pick in Winston's sun since it reflects summer heat and won't bake out like asphalt — see the metal roof cost guide.

The banana-belt cycle is hard on shingles

Winston's warm micro-climate means hot, dry summers that bake asphalt — UV drives out the oils that keep shingles flexible — followed by a wet winter that soaks the now-brittle granules until they wash into the gutters. That accelerates aging compared with cooler, shadier towns. Homes along the South Umpqua and toward Lookingglass also get tree shade on north planes, which brings moss and clogged gutters into the mix. The takeaway: in Winston especially, pay for architectural shingles with algae-resistant granules — the line we install most — not bargain 3-tab that surrenders to the sun in a decade.

What moves your number

The usual levers apply: roof size (everything scales per square), pitch ($475/square walkable up to $775/square steep, 9/12+ custom), tear-off layers, deck repair (priced per sheet before tear-off), complexity (valleys, dormers, chimneys), and material tier. In sun-exposed Winston, ventilation matters too — a poorly vented attic bakes shingles from below and shortens their life, so we check it during every inspection. Our attic ventilation guide explains why.

Permits and the no-overlay rule

Winston falls under Douglas County building rules: reroofs over 100 square feet generally require a permit, and overlays aren't allowed, so every replacement is a full tear-off to the deck. We pull the permit as part of the job. See the permit guide and the county-wide new roof cost guide for context.
