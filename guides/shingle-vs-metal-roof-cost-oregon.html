Shingle vs metal roof cost in Oregon: the real 2026 breakdown.

A metal roof costs 2-3x what shingles cost — but the sticker price only tells half the story. Here's what each really costs per square and in total for a Douglas County home, exactly what drives the gap, and the math that tells you which is smarter for your situation.

⚡ Quick answer

In Douglas County, a full architectural asphalt shingle replacement commonly runs about $10,000-$25,000, while a comparable metal roof typically costs 2-3x that — roughly $25,000-$50,000+. Per square, plan on about $450-$700 for installed asphalt and two-to-three times that for metal. Labor and fabrication, not just the metal itself, drive the gap. On a cost-per-year basis the two land closer than the stickers suggest, because metal lasts 40-70 years to asphalt's 20-25. Every number here is an estimate — a written estimate for your roof is the real figure.

This is the most common money question we get from Roseburg to Myrtle Creek, and it deserves real numbers instead of a national average that ignores our rain, moss, and no-overlay rule. We install both materials with equal seriousness, so we have no reason to nudge you toward the pricier one. Here's the honest breakdown.

What actually drives the 2-3x gap

Homeowners assume the price difference is all in the metal. It isn't. Labor and fabrication are the bigger drivers.

  • Material. Steel or aluminum panels and their coatings cost more than asphalt shingles per square — but this is only part of it.
  • Skilled labor. Metal is measured, cut, seamed, and fastened with precision. It installs slower than nailing shingles and demands a more specialized crew.
  • Custom trim and flashing. Ridge caps, valleys, eave and rake trim, and penetration flashings are often fabricated to the roof. Every valley and dormer adds fabrication.
  • Complexity multiplier. On a cut-up roof with lots of valleys and penetrations, metal's labor premium grows while asphalt handles the complexity gracefully — one reason we sometimes steer complex roofs toward asphalt. Our standing seam vs corrugated guide covers how the panel system itself changes the price.

The hidden local cost most comparisons skip

Sticker-price comparisons leave out the recurring cost that matters most in the Umpqua Valley: moss management on asphalt. Our wet, shaded climate makes moss the number one killer of asphalt roofs, so an honest asphalt cost includes periodic moss treatment across the roof's life. Metal largely deletes that line item because moss can't grip its smooth surface. When you fold ongoing moss care into the asphalt column, the true lifetime gap narrows — which is exactly why the cost-per-year math below matters more than the upfront number.

Cost-per-year: the number that actually decides it

Take a hypothetical Douglas County roof (estimates only — your written estimate is the real math):

  • Asphalt: ~$16,000 lasting ~22 years with maintenance → roughly $730/year, plus moss treatments.
  • Metal: ~$38,000 lasting ~50 years → roughly $760/year, near-zero maintenance.

Per year of service, they're nearly the same roof. The difference is when and how many times you pay. Metal is one big payment now; asphalt is smaller payments spread across two or three replacements at whatever future decades cost. If this is your forever home with a simple roofline, metal's math is compelling. Tighter budget, complex roof, or selling within a decade? Quality asphalt wins. Which is smarter for you?

Choose asphalt if the budget is fixed, the roofline is complex, or you may sell within 5-10 years — a well-installed Class A architectural roof with algae-resistant granules is a genuinely good roof, not a compromise. Choose metal if you're in your forever home, the roof is relatively simple, and you'd happily pay once to never roof again while shedding the moss cycle. Resale, financing, and the permit line

Two cost angles rarely make the sticker comparison, and both matter in Douglas County. First, resale. A new asphalt roof is "neutral" money — it removes a buyer objection and inspection flag but rarely adds a premium, since buyers expect a sound roof. A metal roof can be a genuine selling point ("never roof again") on the right home, though you seldom recoup its full premium at closing. If you're weighing a sale within a few years, that argues for asphalt; if you're settling in, metal's durability is worth more to you than to a future buyer.

Second, the permit and tear-off line. Because Douglas County requires a full tear-off with no overlays and a permit on reroofs over 100 square feet (see our permits guide), both materials carry disposal and permit cost here that overlay-friendly regions skip. That's already baked into our ranges, but it's why a local quote runs higher than a national calculator — and why a bid that mysteriously omits the permit should worry you. A clear, written scope that names the tear-off, the permit, the underlayment, and the flashing is the only way to compare a shingle bid against a metal bid honestly; our how to read a roofing estimate guide walks through it line by line.

Quick answers

How much more is metal than shingles here?

Roughly 2–3x. Asphalt commonly runs $10,000–$25,000 in Douglas County; comparable metal often lands $25,000–$50,000+.

What's the per-square cost?

About $450–$700 per square installed for architectural asphalt; metal runs two-to-three times that, with standing seam costing more than panel.

Why is metal so much more?

Material plus, mostly, labor — precise fabrication, custom trim and flashing, and slower skilled installation. Complex roofs widen the gap.

Any way to lower the cost now?

Match material to your ownership horizon, compare written bids apples-to-apples, and use our current offer: $500 off every new roof plus free whole-home gutters on qualifying $20k+ roofs.
