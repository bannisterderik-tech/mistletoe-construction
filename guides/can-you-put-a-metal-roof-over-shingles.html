Metal Roofing

Can you put a metal roof over shingles?

It's one of the most common questions we get in Douglas County — and the short version is: sometimes it's technically allowed, but in our wet climate it's rarely the right call. Here's why a clean tear-off protects a roof you want to last 50 years.

⚡ Quick answer

In some situations a metal roof can be installed over one layer of shingles — but in Douglas County we almost always tear off first. The county doesn't permit shingle-over-shingle layering, and even where a metal-over-shingle assembly is allowed, our 30-plus inches of annual rain make it a bad gamble. A tear-off lets us inspect and repair the wood deck — exactly where western Oregon moisture hides its damage — so the metal roof you're paying to last decades sits on a sound, dry foundation. The tear-off is modest money that protects the whole investment.

We get this question every week, usually from a homeowner who's heard a metal roof can just go "right over the old one" and save a bundle. It's a fair thing to ask — nobody wants to pay for demolition they don't need. But roofing in the Umpqua Valley isn't the same as roofing in Phoenix, and the honest answer here is shaped by our climate as much as by code.

What the rules actually say

Douglas County follows the state building code, which does not allow stacking a new layer of shingles over old ones — a full reroof means a tear-off to the deck. Metal is a slightly different case: some metal-over-shingle assemblies exist and are permitted in certain jurisdictions and configurations. But "technically allowed somewhere" and "smart for your house" are two different things, and a permit and inspection still apply. We pull the permit on every reroof and build to code — our Douglas County roofing permit guide covers the specifics.

The real reason: your deck

Here's what going over the old shingles really costs you — visibility. The wood sheathing under your roof is where our climate does its quiet damage. Thirty-plus inches of rain a year, moss holding moisture against shaded north slopes, and old leaks that wicked into the deck all leave the plywood soft, delaminated, or rotting in spots you can't see from on top of the shingles. Cover that with metal and the problem doesn't stop — it keeps working, unseen, under a roof you expected to forget about for 50 years.

A tear-off ends the guessing. We strip to bare deck, walk every square foot, replace anything soft, and lay fresh synthetic underlayment before a single metal panel goes down. That's the difference between a metal roof that lasts its full life and one that traps a problem underneath it.

Moisture is the whole story here

In a dry state, layering roofs is a defensible shortcut. In the wet Umpqua Valley, trapped moisture between old shingles and new metal is a recipe for rot and condensation. Our climate is exactly why the "just go over it" advice from a national forum doesn't translate to Douglas County.

Warranties and workmanship

There's a paperwork reason too. Many metal panel manufacturers require installation over a solid, inspected deck with proper underlayment, and some void the 30-to-40-year material warranty if panels are laid over old roofing or on battens above shingles. Since a big part of metal's value is that long warranty, undermining it to save a little on tear-off makes no sense. We install to the panel maker's spec so your coverage stays intact — more on lifespan and warranties in our how long does a metal roof last guide.

What the tear-off adds — and why it's worth it

The tear-off and disposal is a real line item, but a modest one against the cost of a full metal roof. Weigh it against what it buys: a deck you know is sound, underlayment that's fresh, warranties that hold, and no hidden rot working under a roof meant to outlast you in the house. Quick answers

So it's never okay to go over shingles?

Rarely worth it here. In our wet climate, the small savings aren't worth hiding the deck. We tear off and inspect so the metal roof sits on something we've actually verified.

Will a tear-off make my project take longer?

Only slightly. Most homes tear off and dry-in the same day, and it's time well spent finding and fixing any deck issues before the metal goes on.

What if my deck turns out to be fine?

Then great — we confirmed it, replaced nothing extra, and your metal roof starts on a clean, sound base. You only pay for sheathing we actually replace.
