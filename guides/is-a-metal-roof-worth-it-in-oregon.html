Is a metal roof worth it in Oregon?

Metal costs two to three times more than asphalt upfront — so is it worth it in the Umpqua Valley? Here's the honest math on lifespan, moss, energy, and resale from a Douglas County crew that installs both.

By Mistletoe Construction Oregon CCB #255729 Updated August 2026 8 min read

⚡ Quick answer

A metal roof is usually worth it in Oregon if you plan to stay in the home long-term. It costs roughly 2–3× more than architectural asphalt upfront, but lasts 40–70 years versus 18–25 for asphalt here, and its smooth surface sheds our heavy moss and rain far better. Over a 50-year span you'd install two or three asphalt roofs to equal one metal roof. If you may sell within 10–15 years, quality asphalt typically makes more financial sense.

This is the question we get most from Douglas County homeowners who've done a little reading: metal lasts forever and never grows moss, but it costs a fortune — is it actually worth the premium? The honest answer is "it depends on you," and this guide gives you the specific factors that tip the decision, using real Umpqua Valley conditions rather than a national brochure.

Start with the real cost gap

Locally, an architectural asphalt replacement commonly runs $10,000–$25,000 (our Roseburg cost guide has the detail). A comparable metal roof runs roughly 2–3× that, so it often lands in the higher five figures depending on roof size, pitch, panel type, and complexity. Standing-seam (concealed fastener) costs more than exposed-fastener panels — the two styles are compared in our standing seam vs. corrugated guide. That gap is the whole debate. Everything below is about whether the extra money buys you enough.

Lifespan: where metal earns its keep

Here's the math that matters. In western Oregon's wet-dry-moss cycle, asphalt lasts about 18–25 years (see how long a roof lasts here). Metal lasts 40–70. So over the ~50 years you might own a family home:

  • Asphalt path: install now, replace around year 22, and you may be eyeing a third roof before year 50 — roughly two to three full replacements.
  • Metal path: install once, and you're likely done for the life you'll live in the house.

Add tear-off, disposal, and inflation to those repeat asphalt jobs and the "2–3× more expensive" metal roof narrows dramatically on a long enough timeline. The catch is you pay it all upfront, and you only capture the value if you stay. This is the single biggest factor: how long will you own this home?

Moss and maintenance: the local advantage

This is the factor a national guide undersells and Douglas County homeowners feel most. Our shaded, damp, north-facing slopes grow moss aggressively — asphalt roofs here often need moss removal every few years, and moss that's ignored shortens shingle life (our moss prevention guide explains why). A smooth metal roof gives moss, fir needles, and leaves almost nothing to grip, and sheds them with the rain. For a homeowner tired of the moss cycle, that lower maintenance is a genuine, recurring dollar value — not just a nicety.

Shade is the deciding condition

If your roof is heavily shaded by Douglas firs or faces north — common around Glide, Winston, and the wooded lots outside Roseburg — metal's moss-shedding advantage is worth the most. On a wide-open, sun-baked roof, asphalt has an easier life and the metal premium is harder to justify.

The verdict — who should say yes

Metal is worth it if: you plan to stay 15+ years (ideally forever), your roof is shaded and moss-prone, you want to stop thinking about roofing for good, or fire resistance is a priority for your location.

Asphalt is the smarter money if: you may move within 10–15 years, your budget is tight this year, or your roof is simple and sun-exposed where quality architectural shingles will comfortably reach their full life. A word on where you live in the county

Location inside Douglas County nudges the decision too. Homes tucked into the timbered draws around Glide, Idleyld Park, and the Cascade foothills sit under heavy fir canopy and stay damp and needle-covered — the exact conditions where metal's shedding pays off most, and where wildfire resistance is worth the most. More open, sun-exposed lots out toward Winston, Green, and the valley floor around Roseburg give asphalt an easier, longer life, so the metal premium is harder to justify there. Our crew works out of Riddle and covers the whole county, so we've seen how the same roof material ages differently ten miles apart — that local read is part of what a free inspection gives you. And whichever way you lean, a proper install matters more than the metal itself: solid decking, correct underlayment, and clean flashing are what make a metal roof deliver its 50 years, exactly as covered in our roof replacement scope guide.

Quick answers

Is metal worth the extra cost?

If you're staying long-term or fighting a shaded, mossy roof, usually yes over the full lifespan. If you'll move within 10–15 years, asphalt typically wins on dollars.

How much more than asphalt?

Roughly 2–3× upfront. Local asphalt runs about $10k–$25k, so metal often lands in the higher five figures depending on the roof.

Are metal roofs loud in the rain?

No — over solid decking and insulation they're comparable to shingles inside. The loud sound is open-frame metal, not how we install homes.

Does metal really stop the moss problem?

It hugely reduces it. Smooth metal sheds moss and needles that cling to asphalt, which is a real maintenance saving on shaded Umpqua Valley roofs.
