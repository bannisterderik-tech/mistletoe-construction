Roof Inspections

How long does a roof inspection take?

Most take under an hour and a half — but a thorough one covers a lot of ground, from the shingle field to the attic. Here's what our Riddle-based crew actually checks on a Douglas County roof, and what makes an inspection quick or slow.

⚡ Quick answer

A typical residential roof inspection takes about 45 to 90 minutes. A simple single-story roof in decent shape can be done in well under an hour; a large, steep, complex roof with an attic check and a moss problem can run close to two hours. That window covers time on the roof, in the attic when accessible, at the gutters, and a walkthrough of the findings. Ours is free across Douglas County, with photos of everything and an honest call — maintain, repair, or replace.

"Will this take all day?" is one of the first things homeowners ask when they book an inspection — usually because they're picturing a big disruption. It isn't. A roof inspection is a fast, focused visit, and a good one tells you exactly where your roof stands without any pressure. Here's how long it really takes, what we're doing during that time, and what stretches it out on Umpqua Valley homes.

The typical time: 45 to 90 minutes

For most homes in Douglas County, plan on somewhere between three-quarters of an hour and an hour and a half. That's the honest average across the roofs we look at from Roseburg to Canyonville. A tidy single-story ranch with a simple gable roof might take 40 minutes; a rambling two-story with dormers, multiple valleys, an attic to crawl, and moss to assess can push toward two hours. The point of the visit isn't speed — it's making sure nothing gets missed — but it's rarely the all-day event people fear.

What makes it longer or shorter

  • Roof size and complexity. More planes, dormers, and valleys mean more to walk and check.
  • Pitch and access. Steep, wet, mossy Oregon roofs slow careful footing; sometimes we assess portions from a ladder and drone rather than walking every plane.
  • Attic access. A reachable attic adds a valuable interior check but takes extra time — and it's worth it.
  • Condition. A roof with real problems takes longer to document properly than a clean one.
  • Moss and debris. Heavy moss common in our shaded neighborhoods needs closer assessment to judge whether the shingle surface is still sound.
  • Weather. We won't walk a roof in a downpour or high wind; we schedule around the dry windows.

What we actually check

A real inspection is more than a glance from the driveway. Here's the ground we cover:

  • The shingle field — granule loss, curling, cupping, cracking, and bald spots.
  • Flashing — chimneys, sidewalls, valleys, and every penetration, the spots that leak first. Our flashing guide shows what good looks like.
  • Pipe boots and vents — cracked rubber boots are a top leak source.
  • Gutters and downspouts — buildup, sagging, and granule sludge that signals shingle wear.
  • The attic, when accessible — daylight through the sheathing, damp or stained wood, and ventilation, one of the most honest tests of a roof's health.
  • Moss and deck — coverage, and any soft or sagging areas underfoot that hint at moisture damage.

Our full roof inspection checklist lays out every item in detail, and our signs you need a new roof guide explains what the findings mean.

Why you shouldn't do it yourself

Our roofs are steep, wet, and slick with moss for much of the year, and ladder or roof falls are exactly why homeowners shouldn't walk their own roof to inspect it. Leave the climbing to a crew that's tied off and does it daily. The attic-and-ground checks you can do safely; the on-roof part is ours.

What to expect at your inspection

When we come out, we'll get on the roof, work through the checklist, and photograph everything we find — good and bad. You don't need to hover or be on the roof with us. At the end we sit down and walk you through the photos with a straight recommendation: maintain it, make a targeted repair, or plan a replacement. If your roof has years of life left, we'll say so — roof repair is a service we're glad to sell, so we have no reason to oversell a new roof. You're left with documentation and an honest read, not a hard pitch.

How often to have it done

Once a year is the right cadence in Douglas County — ideally in fall before the heavy rains — plus a look after any significant windstorm. Roofs over about 15 years, or homes under heavy fir and oak cover, should never skip the annual check. Quick answers

Can an inspection be done in the rain?

We won't walk a roof in a downpour or high wind for safety and accuracy. We schedule around our dry windows, which the Umpqua Valley gives us even in winter.

Will you check the attic too?

Yes, when it's accessible — the attic check for daylight, damp wood, and ventilation is one of the most telling parts of the inspection.

How soon can you come out?

We serve all of Douglas County from Riddle, so scheduling is usually quick, and we prioritize active leaks. Call (541) 670-5005.

Do I get anything in writing?

You get photos of the findings and, if work is warranted, an itemized written estimate — no verbal-only guesses.
