Roof replacement cost in Sutherlin, Oregon.

From the mill-era homes near downtown to the new subdivisions off the I-5 corridor, Sutherlin's roofs run the full range — and so do their prices. Here are the honest 2026 numbers, tied to roof size, pitch, and material.

Most asphalt-shingle roof replacements in Sutherlin run roughly $10,000–$25,000 with a full tear-off — about $475–$775 per square of architectural shingles, depending on pitch. Sutherlin has grown from a timber-mill town into one of Douglas County's busier I-5 corridor communities, and its rooftops tell that story: older, compact homes near the center, and sprawling newer subdivisions spreading toward the edges of town. Those two housing styles price very differently, which is exactly why a single "average roof cost" number is useless here. 2026 Sutherlin price ranges

  • Architectural asphalt: most homes land $10,000–$25,000, full tear-off included. Small downtown-era homes can dip below; large subdivision homes with steep or complex roofs go above.
  • Per square (100 sq ft): roughly $475–$775 installed — low end for walkable ranch pitches, high end for steep, harness-required roofs common on newer two-story builds.
  • Metal: about 1.9× asphalt, increasingly popular on the newer builds — see the metal roof cost guide.

Two housing stocks, two cost pictures

Sutherlin's split personality really does move pricing:

  • Mill-era homes near downtown tend to be smaller (lower total cost) but are more likely to hide aging decks or a layered old roof — meaning tear-off and possible sheathing replacement. An honest bid states a per-sheet deck price before tear-off.
  • Newer I-5 corridor subdivisions are larger with more square footage and often steeper, cut-up rooflines — more squares and higher pitch push the total up, but the decking is usually sound.

Either way, the two biggest levers are the same everywhere: roof size and pitch.

What Sutherlin roofs are up against

Sutherlin gets the same wet-side pattern as the rest of the Umpqua Valley — a long, wet October-to-May stretch followed by hot, dry summers — and that cycle ages asphalt faster than the national average. Homes near Ford's Pond and the tree-lined older streets deal with more shade, which means moss on north planes and gutters full of fir and oak litter. The newer subdivisions on open ground catch more sun and wind, which fades and lifts shingles over time. Architectural shingles with algae-resistant granules — the line we install most — stand up to both.

Permits and the no-overlay rule

Sutherlin falls under Douglas County building rules: reroofs over 100 square feet generally require a permit, and overlays aren't allowed, so every replacement is a full tear-off to the deck. We pull the permit as part of the job. See the permit guide and the county-wide new roof cost guide for the bigger picture.
