The river-bottom challenge

Myrtle Creek sits where its namesake creek meets the South Umpqua River, and that low, tree-lined river bottom is a moss factory. Shaded north-facing roof planes stay damp for months, growing the moss that lifts shingle edges and holds water against the deck; valleys and gutters fill with fir and oak litter; and morning river fog keeps everything wet longer than up on open ground. Then summer flips to hot and dry, baking the granules. Homes up on the hillsides trade damp for wind exposure along their ridgelines. Architectural shingles with algae-resistant granules — the line we install most — are built for exactly this.

What moves your number

The usual levers: roof size (everything scales per square), pitch ($475/square walkable up to $775/square steep, 9/12+ custom), tear-off layers, deck repair (river-bottom homes with long-term moss sometimes hide soft sheathing — priced per sheet before tear-off), complexity, and material tier. Because damp river-bottom roofs are more prone to hidden deck issues, an honest bid here always states a per-sheet deck repair price up front.

Permits and the no-overlay rule

Myrtle Creek falls under Douglas County building rules: reroofs over 100 square feet generally require a permit, and overlays aren't allowed, so every replacement is a full tear-off to the deck. We pull the permit as part of the job. See the permit guide and the county-wide new roof cost guide for context.
