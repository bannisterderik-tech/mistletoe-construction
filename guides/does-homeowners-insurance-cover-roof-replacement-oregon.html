Does homeowners insurance cover roof replacement in Oregon?

Sometimes yes, sometimes no — and the difference usually comes down to one word: cause. Here's how Oregon policies treat roofs, what ACV vs. replacement cost really means for your check, and how a Douglas County claim actually plays out.

⚡ Quick answer

Oregon homeowners insurance generally covers roof replacement when the damage is caused by a sudden covered peril — wind, hail, a fallen tree, or an ice storm — after you pay your deductible. It generally does not cover a roof that simply wore out from age, moss, or neglect; that's considered maintenance. Whether you get a depreciated (ACV) check or full replacement-cost value depends on your policy language. A free, honest inspection tells you which situation you're in before you file.

Every Douglas County winter, the phone rings the morning after a windstorm: "A branch came through the roof — will insurance pay for it?" And a few weeks later, a different call: "My roof's just old and leaking everywhere — same question?" Those two homeowners get very different answers, and understanding why saves a lot of frustration.

We're roofers, not insurance agents, and your policy is the final word — read it or call your agent. But we've documented enough Umpqua Valley claims to explain how this usually works.

It all comes down to cause: storm vs. wear

Homeowners policies are built to cover sudden, accidental damage from specific "perils," not the slow march of time. In our climate the covered events that damage roofs are mostly:

  • Wind. The winter windstorms that roll off the Coast Range and funnel through the valley can lift or tear shingles, especially on older 3-tab roofs. Wind is the most common covered roof peril here.
  • Falling trees and limbs. Douglas-fir and oak branches come down in saturated soil and high wind — a very common Douglas County claim. Impact damage is typically covered.
  • Ice and winter storms. The Umpqua Valley doesn't see heavy snow most years, but hard ice storms do happen and can cause covered damage, particularly in higher spots like Glide and the Cascade foothills.
  • Hail. Less common here than in the Midwest, but it does occur and can bruise shingles.

What's almost never covered: age, wear, moss and algae growth, granule loss, poor installation, and neglect. If your roof is 25 years old and finally giving up, insurance sees that as your maintenance responsibility, not a claim. Our guide on how long a roof lasts in Oregon and moss removal covers keeping wear from ending your roof early.

💡 The gray area: storm damage on an aging roof

The tricky claims are when a storm hits a roof that was already worn. Insurers may argue the roof "would have failed anyway." Good documentation — dated photos, the storm event, the specific damage pattern — is what separates an approved claim from a denied one. That's the single biggest thing a careful contractor adds.

ACV vs. replacement cost: the number that surprises people

Even on a covered claim, how much you get depends on your policy type:

Actual Cash Value (ACV)Replacement Cost Value (RCV)
What it paysThe depreciated value — new cost minus age and wear.The full cost to install a new roof.
Older roof impactBig depreciation deduction; a small check on a 20-year roof.Age doesn't shrink the final payout.
How it's paidOne depreciated payment.Initial ACV payment, then withheld "recoverable depreciation" released after the work is completed and invoiced.

Many Oregon insurers have shifted older roofs to ACV-only coverage in recent years, which can leave a homeowner with a modest check and a large gap to a real replacement. Check your policy for the words "replacement cost" on the roof before a storm hits — it's much harder to change after damage occurs. This RCV two-part payment is also why the deal-tracking on a storm job includes a second invoice once depreciation is released.

Deductibles and the "is it worth filing?" math

You pay your deductible; insurance covers the rest of a covered loss. Many Umpqua Valley homeowners carry a flat $1,000–$2,500 deductible, though some policies apply a percentage deductible to wind or storm losses, which can be larger than you expect on a higher-valued home. If the repair estimate is only a little above your deductible, filing may not make sense — you'd spend a claim on your record for a small net payout. During a free inspection we'll give you the honest read on whether a claim is worth opening at all.

How a Douglas County roof claim actually goes

  1. Document immediately. After a storm, take dated photos from the ground, note the date and event, and tarp active leaks to prevent further damage (insurers expect you to mitigate). Our roof-leak emergency guide covers the first hours.
  2. Get a professional inspection. We get on the roof, photograph everything, and tell you whether the damage is genuinely storm-related and claim-worthy — before you call it in.
  3. File with your insurer. You open the claim; an adjuster is assigned. We'll meet the adjuster on the roof to walk through the damage together so nothing gets missed.
  4. Review the scope and payment. Confirm whether it's ACV or RCV, what's covered, and your deductible.
  5. We replace the roof. Full tear-off (Douglas County doesn't allow overlays), permit pulled, and documentation for the depreciation release. See our storm-damage claims deep dive for the detailed walkthrough.

⚠ Never let anyone offer to "waive your deductible"

Storm-chaser crews sweep through Roseburg after big windstorms promising to eat your deductible or handle the whole claim for you. Waiving a deductible is insurance fraud in Oregon, and these crews vanish before the first callback. Quick answers

Will insurance replace my whole roof or just the damaged section?

It depends on the damage and your policy. Adjusters often approve partial repairs, but if matching shingles are unavailable or damage is widespread, a full replacement may be warranted. Douglas County's no-overlay rule means covered work is done as a proper tear-off.

How long do I have to file after an Oregon storm?

Report promptly — policies expect timely notice and reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Don't wait through the winter; document and call your insurer soon after the event.

Is moss or wear ever covered?

No. Moss, algae, granule loss, and age are maintenance, not covered perils. Regular moss removal and inspections keep wear from ending your roof early.

Should a contractor inspect before I file?

Yes. A free, honest inspection tells you whether the damage is storm-related and worth a claim, so you're not opening one that gets denied. We document it either way.
