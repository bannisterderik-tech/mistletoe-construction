Metal Roofing

How long does a metal roof last?

In the wet Umpqua Valley, a metal roof is the closest thing to a buy-it-once decision. Here's the realistic lifespan — 40 to 70 years — what stretches or shortens it in our climate, and how it stacks up against asphalt over the long haul.

⚡ Quick answer

A properly installed standing-seam metal roof lasts 40 to 70 years in western Oregon, and frequently longer. Exposed-fastener panels run about 30 to 45 years — the rubber washers on the screws wear out before the metal does. Either way, in the wet Umpqua Valley a single metal roof typically outlives two or even three asphalt roofs. What decides where you land is installation quality and flashing details, not the rain.

Metal roofing is having a moment across Douglas County, and for good reason. When you live under 30-plus inches of annual rain and watch asphalt shingles wear out early, a roof that measures its life in half-centuries starts to look like the smart money. We install metal on homes from the ranches around Riddle and Canyonville to the hillside houses above Roseburg, and the first question is almost always: how long will it really last here?

The real numbers by system

  • Standing seam (concealed fasteners): 40–70 years, often more. The seams lock together with no screw holes through the face of the metal, so there's simply less to fail. This is the long-life option.
  • Exposed-fastener / corrugated panels: 30–45 years. Excellent value, but the neoprene washers under the screws are the wear point — plan to inspect and eventually re-gasket them over the roof's life.
  • Stone-coated steel: 40–50+ years, with a shingle-like look many Umpqua Valley homeowners prefer over ribbed panels.

Compare that to asphalt, which in our climate realistically gives 18–25 years — see our roof lifespan in Oregon guide and the head-to-head in asphalt vs. metal roof lifespan. On the difference between panel types, our standing seam vs. corrugated guide lays out the trade-offs.

Why metal loves the Umpqua Valley climate

The same wet-then-hot cycle that punishes asphalt barely touches metal. Asphalt fails because it soaks up water, grows moss on shaded north slopes, and then bakes brittle in July and August. Metal does none of that. Water runs off a smooth panel instead of sitting on it, so the constant Pacific rain that ages shingles is a non-event. Moss struggles to anchor on metal, which matters enormously on the shaded, tree-canopied lots common around Roseburg and Myrtle Creek. And metal doesn't dry out and crack in the summer sun the way an asphalt mat does.

There's a wildfire angle too. Since the 2020 fires burned through the North Umpqua corridor, more Douglas County homeowners want ignition-resistant roofing, and a metal roof as a Class A assembly sheds embers well. Our wildfire-resistant roofing guide covers the assemblies that matter here.

The one thing that shortens a metal roof

It's almost never the metal — it's the install. Bad flashing at valleys and walls, panels cut with an abrasive saw that throws rusting filings, dissimilar metals left touching, or screws driven crooked. A quality panel installed by a crew that knows the details is one of the last roofs you'll ever buy. Installed wrong, even great metal leaks in five years.

What stretches or shortens the number

  • Coating and metal choice. Kynar/PVDF paint finishes and heavier-gauge coated steel or aluminum hold color and resist corrosion far longer than budget panels.
  • Flashing quality. Valleys, sidewalls, chimneys, and penetrations are where any roof leaks first. Done right in metal, they outlast everything around them.
  • Fastener type. Concealed-clip standing seam has no face penetrations to age; exposed fasteners need periodic attention.
  • Coastal salt air. We're inland in the Umpqua Valley, so salt corrosion is far less of a factor than on the coast — an advantage for our metal roofs.
  • Debris and maintenance. Keeping needles and leaves off the panels and out of the valleys under Douglas-fir canopy helps any roof last.

Is the lifespan worth the price?

Metal costs roughly two to three times what asphalt does up front. For a homeowner planning to stay put, the math often works anyway: paying once for 50 years beats paying two or three times for 20-year asphalt roofs, plus you skip the tear-off labor and disruption each time. Quick answers

Will a metal roof really last 50 years here?

A quality standing-seam roof installed correctly, yes — 40 to 70 years is the realistic band in western Oregon, and many go longer with minimal upkeep.

Do metal roofs rust in Oregon rain?

Quality coated steel and aluminum panels don't rust from rain. Rust usually traces to cheap uncoated metal or filings left from cutting panels with the wrong blade — an installation problem, not a rain problem.

Is metal noisy in the rain?

Over a solid deck with underlayment — how we install it — a metal roof is no louder than any other. The tin-roof-on-a-barn sound comes from panels over open purlins, not a modern home install.
