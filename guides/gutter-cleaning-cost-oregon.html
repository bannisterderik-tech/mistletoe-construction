Gutters & Cost

Gutter cleaning cost in Oregon.

In the Umpqua Valley, gutters fill with fir needles and oak leaves faster than almost anywhere. Here's what a cleaning actually costs in Douglas County, what pushes the price up or down, and why it's a twice-a-year job here.

Gutter cleaning in Douglas County typically runs about $150–$250 for a single-story home and $200–$350 for a two-story, depending on gutter length, roof height and pitch, and how packed the debris is. Gutters are boring right up until they aren't — and in the wet Umpqua Valley, clogged gutters cause a surprising share of the water damage we get called about. This guide lays out honest pricing for gutter cleaning across Douglas County, what makes one house cost more than another, and how to decide between paying for regular cleanings and installing guards.

Typical gutter cleaning prices in Douglas County

Pricing is usually based on the size of the home and how hard the access is. As a working range for our area:

  • Single-story home: roughly $150–$250 per cleaning.
  • Two-story home: roughly $200–$350, since height and ladder work slow it down.
  • Large, steep, or heavily wooded homes: more, because packed needles and difficult access take longer and demand more care.

These are ballparks, not quotes. The only real number comes from looking at your roofline, gutter run, and debris load. What you're paying for is not just scooping leaves — it's clearing the downspouts all the way through, bagging or hauling the debris, and doing it safely on wet, mossy Oregon roofs.

What drives the price up or down

Several things move a gutter-cleaning quote:

  • Home height and roof pitch. Two- and three-story homes and steep pitches mean taller ladders, slower work, and more risk — all of which cost more.
  • Total length of gutter. A rambling ranch with long runs and multiple downspouts is more work than a compact two-story footprint.
  • How much debris, and how packed. Fir needles compact into a dense mat that's slow to clear; a light leaf load clears fast.
  • Tree cover. Homes buried under Douglas-fir, oak, or big-leaf maple fill faster and often need clearing on the roof itself, not just the gutters.
  • Downspout clogs. A plugged downspout or underground drain line takes extra work to clear.
  • Add-ons. Minor gutter repairs, resecuring loose hangers, or a roof debris blow-off may be bundled in.

Why it's a twice-a-year job in the Umpqua Valley

Our climate and canopy make gutter cleaning a bigger deal here than in a dry, open region. Douglas-fir sheds needles year-round, oaks and maples dump leaves every fall, and the moss grit that washes off aging shingles settles into the troughs. Combine that debris with 30-plus inches of annual rain, and a clogged gutter doesn't just overflow — it sends water behind the fascia board, down the siding, and toward the foundation. That's how a $200 cleaning turns into rotted fascia, stained siding, and even basement moisture. Homes under heavy fir cover — think the wooded lots around Glide and the tree-lined streets of Roseburg's Hucrest — often need clearing more than twice a year. The real cost of skipping it

Overflowing gutters are one of the most common causes of the fascia rot, siding damage, and foundation moisture we see across Douglas County. The cleaning is cheap insurance. The repairs it prevents — rotted trim, water-stained siding, even interior leaks — are not.

DIY vs. hiring it out

Cleaning your own gutters costs only your time and a ladder — but that's the catch. Our roofs are frequently two stories, wet, and slick with moss for much of the year, and ladder falls onto concrete are exactly why most homeowners eventually hand this off. A professional cleaning also includes checking the downspouts, spotting loose fasteners and small leaks before they grow, and doing the whole thing from proper footing. If you're comfortable and your home is a low single-story, DIY is reasonable. If you're eyeing a wet two-story in January, it isn't worth the risk.

Quick answers

What's a fair price for a two-story home?

Roughly $200–$350 in Douglas County, depending on gutter length, pitch, and debris. Heavy fir buildup or difficult access pushes it higher.

How long does a cleaning take?

A typical single-family home takes one to two hours, longer for large, steep, or heavily clogged homes with plugged downspouts.

Do you clean the downspouts too?

Yes — a proper cleaning clears the downspouts through to the outlet, not just the troughs. A clear gutter that drains into a clogged downspout still overflows.
